How to solve one
  • Draw: drag across cells to make a rectangle. Tap a rectangle to remove it. Tap a number to see where it could go.
  • Start with 1s. A 1 is a single cell. Tap it to place it.
  • List the shapes. A number's rectangles come from its factor pairs. A 6 can be 1×6, 2×3, 3×2 or 6×1, then the grid edge and nearby numbers remove most of them.
  • Primes are strips. A 5, 7 or 11 can only be one row or one column.
  • Find forced cells. Tap a number and look at the darker cells. Every placement covers them, so you can fill them in.
  • Leave no orphans. Every cell must end up in a rectangle. If only one number can reach a cell, that number has to stretch to it.
  • Hint: outlines in amber, for five seconds, a rectangle you can place now and says why. It does not place it. If one of your rectangles is in the way, it outlines that one in red. See Logic rating and hints below.
  • Do not guess. Each puzzle has one solution that logic can reach.
Logic rating and hints

The Logic rating, next to Time and Hints, says how hard the thinking is, whatever the size. It is the hardest kind of step the puzzle needs.

  • Direct: a number has only one place left.
  • Cross-check: a cell that only one number can reach.
  • Elimination: cells a number must own rule out other options.
  • Trial: you have to test a guess.

Size alone does not decide difficulty: some small puzzles need a trial, and some big ones fall to single-option moves. Practice levels aim for it, one technique each: Easy needs only Direct, Medium goes up to Cross-check, Hard and Expert need Elimination, Master needs Trial. The daily puzzle is chosen by size only; it shows its rating but is not steered and earns no badge.

Badges. Solve a practice puzzle inside all three limits on the bar (finish within twice the par time, and no more than the mistakes and hints allowed) and you earn the badge for that level's technique. Tick marks on the bar show the limits and the ticker shows where you are: green is safe, yellow means you are past par, red means that limit is gone and so is the badge. A mistake is a block that is not part of the solution. Master hides the mistake count until you finish. Medium needs the Direct badge, Hard needs Cross-check, Expert needs Elimination, and Master needs Elimination earned on Expert. Badges show at the bottom of the page. You can switch the locks off in Settings; badges are still kept.

Master is the top size with a puzzle that needs at least two Trial steps. It has no previews when you tap a number, no stripes on wrong rectangles, and only three hints. The counter says Placed, not Correct, because it no longer tells you what is right. Hints work from the same steps. A hint outlines the easiest forced rectangle, says why it is forced and names the technique, for example Hint · Cross-check. For a cross-check it also marks the key cell with a dashed outline. The outline fades after 5 seconds; the explanation stays until your next move. If no step is left, the hint says so and gives you the answer. Each new hint counts.

How the daily puzzle works

Everyone who plays on the same date gets the same puzzle, so you can compare times. The grid grows through the week: 5×5 on Monday up to 13×13 on Saturday. Your streak counts every day you finish the daily, with or without hints. The timer only runs while the page is open and you are drawing, so a closed page pauses it.

How the puzzles are made

The generator cuts the grid into random rectangles and puts one number in each. A solver then counts the solutions, stopping at two. Puzzles with more than one solution are thrown away, so the one you get has exactly one answer.

How to play
  • Look around: drag the cube to rotate it and pinch to zoom. Front, Right and Top jump to straight-on views.
  • Pick a number: tap it in the cube or in the list. Numbers that overlap on screen take turns when you tap again.
  • Choose a box: the cube keeps rotating freely while you preview, so you can look at the dashed box from any side. Step through placements and shapes with the arrows and chips on the cube. Press Place box when the dashed box is right. Tap Lock if you would rather freeze the cube: then swipe sideways to step through placements and up or down to change shape. The lock stays on until you tap it again.
  • Read the extent: the dashed box has a dot in every cell it fills, a grid on its faces, and a blue shadow on the three walls behind the cube. Count the shadow squares on each wall to get its size along each axis.
  • Read the blue circles: they mark empty cells that the selected number's remaining boxes would cover. A ring means every option covers that cell, so the box must include it.
  • Watch for red: a red number has no room left, and a red ring marks an empty cell that no number can reach. Undo, or remove a box nearby.
  • Finished boxes lose their number in the cube to keep it readable. Their colour stays, and the number list below shows them in the same colour. To take one back, tap its number in the list and press Remove box.
  • Hint outlines in amber, for five seconds, a box you can place now and says why. It does not place it. If one of your boxes is in the way, it outlines that one in red. See Logic rating and hints below.
  • Remaining view hides placed boxes so you can see what is still empty inside the cube.

The generator cuts the cube into random boxes and puts one number in each, equal to the box's volume. A solver then counts the solutions, stopping at two. Goal. Fill the flower with blocks of diamonds. Each block holds exactly one number, and that number is how many diamonds the block has.

The flower. The diamonds lie along two families of spirals, one turning each way. A block is a rectangle between two spirals of each kind: 2×3 means two diamonds along one spiral and three along the other. The ring closes, so a block can cross any petal. The inner and outer edges are zigzags, and a block cannot poke past them.

Controls. Drag from one diamond to another: they are opposite corners of the block. Tap a block to remove it. Tap a number to see where it could go. A 1 places itself when you tap it.

Levels. Size is rings × petals, from 5×8 on Easy up to 10×16 on Master. Easy needs only Direct steps, Medium goes up to Cross-check, Hard and Expert need Elimination, and Master needs Trial steps. Master has no previews, no error marks and only three hints. Bloom has no daily puzzle yet.

A mistake is a block that is not part of the solution. Master hides the mistake count until you finish. Medium needs the Direct badge, Hard needs Cross-check, Expert needs Elimination, and Master needs Elimination earned on Expert. Bloom badges are separate from 2D and 3D. The Level locks switch in Settings turns the locks off.
