How Sudoku works

Fill the grid so that every row, every column and every 3×3 box contains the digits 1 to 9 exactly once. Some cells start filled in. A well-made puzzle has exactly one solution, so logic is always enough and guessing is never required.

A routine that works

  1. Scan for digits that are nearly complete, placed seven or eight times. Their last positions are the easiest to find.
  2. When scanning stops paying off, fill in notes. A cell's notes list the digits still legal there. The Fill notes button shows every candidate for seven seconds, then hides them again, so look quickly (the full rules are under Fill notes below). Write your own notes with the Notes tool to keep them.
  3. Look for patterns in the notes, simplest first: singles, then locked candidates, then pairs.

Fill notes

Tap Fill notes to see every digit that is still legal in every empty cell for seven seconds. It costs one use. Hold the button for half a second to spend two uses and keep the candidates on screen for twenty-one seconds, three times as long; if you only have one use left, a hold gives the normal seven-second look instead. On a keyboard, F is a tap and Shift+F is a hold.

The candidates are worked out live from the board and drawn in place of your own notes. Your notes are never changed or erased, and they come back when the time is up. While the candidates are showing you cannot pencil notes, and a placed digit updates them straight away. The number on the button is how many uses are left, and the thin bar on it runs down as the time passes.

Each puzzle has a fixed number of uses, set by the level. Classic: Beginner 16, Easy 12, Medium 10, Hard 8, Expert 6. Cube 3×3×3: Easy 10, Medium 8, Hard 6, Expert 4. Cube 4×4×4: Easy 12, Medium 10, Hard 8, Expert 6. Restarting a puzzle restores them. Games saved before this change keep the number of uses they had left.

Techniques, simplest first

Naked single Beginner

A cell whose row, column and box already contain eight different digits can only hold the ninth.

Hidden single Easy

If a digit fits in only one cell of a row, column or box, it belongs there, even when that cell also has other candidates.

Locked candidates Medium

If inside a box a digit can only go in one row, then it cannot appear anywhere else in that row. The same works in reverse, from a line into a box.

Naked pair Medium

Two cells in a unit share the same two candidates. Those two digits must fill those two cells, so remove them from the rest of the unit.

Hidden pair and triple Hard

Two digits that fit in only the same two cells of a unit. Those cells cannot hold anything else, so clear their other candidates. Triples follow the same logic.

X-Wing Hard

A digit has exactly two possible cells in each of two rows, and they share the same two columns. The digit must occupy opposite corners, so remove it from the rest of those columns.

Chains and wings Expert

Longer patterns that link candidates across the grid. The Hint button flags these as advanced instead of naming them.

Cube mode

The cube has 27 cells in three layers of 3×3. It has nine slices: three left to right (X), three front to back (Y) and three bottom to top (Z). Every slice must hold the digits 1 to 9 exactly once, so each cell belongs to three slices and a digit placed in one layer rules it out along lines in the other two directions.

Each digit appears exactly three times, always in cells that differ in all three coordinates. Use Slice by to turn the cube and view it along another axis; the same cells, three new layers. Selecting a cell shades everything it shares a slice with, across all layers.

3D cube view. Drag the cube to turn it in any direction, pinch or scroll to zoom, and tap Level to stand it upright again. The small X, Y and Z arrows in the corner show which way each axis counts up. Use the buttons under the cube to snap to Front, Back, Left, Right, Top or Bottom. Tap a square on any side to select it and type its digit. The cube turns freely by default. If a slip of the finger keeps spinning it, tap Lock: dragging then does nothing, and four arrow buttons turn the cube one step at a time (hold one to keep turning). Tap Unlock to turn it freely again. Digits and notes always turn to read the right way up on every face, and Level stands the cube itself upright; switch the digits to follow each face instead in Settings. The one cell buried in the middle cannot be seen from outside: tap Peel to cut away the layer facing you and reach it. While peeled, Front, Back, Left, Right, Top and Bottom choose which side is cut. Flat and Stack remain available.

Flat net. The Flat net button opens a view-only map of your own board. It unfolds the cube into six faces and folds it back, so you can see that a square on an edge or corner is one cell printed on two or three faces. It also highlights a slice or a digit. It cannot be edited and never shows the answer.

4×4×4 cube. The same rules on a bigger cube: 64 cells and twelve slices, each slice a 4×4 layer that must hold 1 to 16 exactly once. Each digit appears four times, never sharing an X, Y or Z. The sixteen digit keys replace the usual nine, and on a keyboard you type 10 to 16 as two keys (1 then 0 to 6). The eight squares in the middle cannot be seen from outside: tap Peel to cut away the layers facing you and reach the hidden 2×2×2 core. While peeled, the side buttons choose which side is cut and the arrows peel one layer deeper. Notes are drawn as dots until you zoom in close enough to read them.

Digit first entry

Turn on Digit first (next to Notes) to enter digits the other way round. Tap a digit on the pad to arm it, then tap squares to place it; every copy of that digit is highlighted on the board, so you can sweep for where it still fits. Tap the digit again, or press Esc, to put it down. With Notes on, tapping squares pencils the armed digit in instead. A dragged cube never places a digit, only a clean tap does. The D key switches the mode on and off.

Marking digits

When you are hunting for the missing digit in a slice, hold the Notes button (or press K) to switch to Mark. The button turns into Mark, and each digit you tap on the pad turns amber and struck through instead of being placed, so the plain keys left are the ones that can still go there. Tap a crossed digit to uncross it. Tap the Mark button once to finish: all the marks clear and the pad is back to normal. Marks are scratch work, are never saved, and never touch your notes. If Notes was on, it is still on when you finish marking.

Last-cell auto-fill

When a row, column, box or cube slice has a single empty cell left, the game fills it for you, and keeps going if that completes another unit. It only fills a digit that is correct, so a unit containing a wrong entry stays untouched. Switch it off in Settings.

How difficulty is set

Each puzzle is graded by a solver that uses human techniques, in order, and records the hardest one it needed. Clue count does not decide the level, because a puzzle with fewer clues is not always harder. Expert puzzles are the ones this solver cannot finish on its own.

Keyboard

1–9 enter a digit. Arrow keys move. Backspace erases. N toggles notes. H gives a hint. Z undoes and Y redoes. P pauses. In cube mode, [ and ] move between layers, and X, Y or Z choose the slicing axis.

Statistics

Times are stored in this browser only. A puzzle counts as played when it is generated.

Out of mistakes

Strict mode ends the game at three mistakes. You can try the same puzzle again or start a different one.
